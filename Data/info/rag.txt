Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) enhances LLM responses using external knowledge sources.
A RAG pipeline typically consists of document ingestion, preprocessing, chunking, embedding, retrieval, and generation.
Document loaders extract content from sources such as PDF, TXT, DOCX, and web pages.
Text chunking divides large documents into smaller sections for efficient retrieval.
RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter is commonly used to split documents while preserving text structure.
Embedding models convert text into numerical vectors representing semantic meaning.
FAISS, ChromaDB, and Pinecone are examples of vector databases used in RAG applications.
Similarity search retrieves document chunks based on their semantic relevance to a query.
A reranker improves retrieval quality by reordering retrieved documents based on relevance.
The retrieved context is passed to an LLM to generate a grounded response.
